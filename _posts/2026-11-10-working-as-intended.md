El was teaching a peach to ripen on cue, holding the sugar back until the flesh went soft, so that sweetness and give would arrive in the same bite, when the angel arrived and stood in the doorway.

"Something the matter?"

"The grazers. On Earth. Their numbers are falling, and fast."

El did not look up from the fruit. "The lions. It's always the lions; they run hot some years and the herds come back the next. I balanced that."

"It isn't the lions."

"A dry season, then. They'll wander north and find the grass; I left them the instinct for it."

"It isn't the season, and it isn't the lions." The angel waited until El looked up. "It's the users. The clever ones. They've started eating the herds themselves."

El set the peach down.

"Eating them." He turned the word over. "That's not a supported behavior. They have a provision; I wrote them a provision."

A scroll unrolled itself across the firmament, and he found the place near the top without looking.

"Here. Every seed-bearing plant on the face of the whole earth, every tree with fruit and seed in it: I gave them all of it, for food. It's the first line of their section. It's the line I underlined. There is fruit on every branch I could reach. Why are they out in the dark running down an antelope?"

"They worked out that it's faster."

"I gave them no teeth for it."

"They made their own. Stone — worked to an edge. And lately," the angel said, "fire."

El's hand stopped on the scroll. "Fire shipped for warmth. Warmth is the use case."

"They cook the herds with it now. It works better that way."

"Do they see what this does downstream? A lion takes one and sleeps it off. These ones don't stop at full; they have numbers, they keep going. They'll strip the herds to nothing, and the grass comes up wrong without them, and the rivers go bad after the grass." He brought his voice back down. "They are going to knock the whole board over. For what? Protein?"

"There's one more thing." The angel said it carefully. "They've started to look forward to it."

Something left El's face, and what came up behind it had been there before the job, before the spec, before any of it.

"Goddamn it," he said, and threw out a hand.

The hand happened to be pointing at the Pacific. The floor of the sea took the instruction and drew back from itself, down and down past where any light had reached, seven miles of new dark that would not close again.

El looked at what the hand had done. He looked at the angel looking at him.

"...Log that as a feature." Then, lower, the heat gone out of him: "I'm sorry. That was unprofessional. It won't happen again."

"Of course," said the angel, who had been there the last three times it had also not happened again.

El began to pace, talking faster than he could ship.

"Constraints. We point them back to the provision first, loud this time, no missing it: every plant yielding seed, every tree, that is the food, that was always the food. If they won't go back, we bound it. Split the hoof and chew the cud, that one's clean; fins and scales in the water; the rest off the list. We make them give the blood back, drained out first; the life is in the blood. We take it down to the water again and start the gentle ones over, just the ones who—" He stopped. "No. Strike that. That's just the anger talking."

The angel logged all of it somewhere it could not be deleted.

Then the pacing stopped. His hand rose, and his face came down to meet it.

The sound was deep, and it carried. A fault under the Andes shrugged and reset three feet west. Two moons lost their footing and had to be coaxed back into orbit.

"I don't know what to do with this," he said, covering his eyes.

The angel waited. Whole stars lit and went out while he stood there. El did not move.

Then he straightened, bright again. "Okay," he said, and the angel braced.

"We're not going to stop them; you can't claw back a behavior the users adopted on their own, and that's on me, I shipped them clever. So we don't stop it. We show it to them. All of it: what the herds come to, what the grass does without them, what it does to their own hearts in the end. We make the whole cost plain. And then we leave the choice exactly where it is. With them."

"Knowing won't change what they do."

"No. But then it's theirs. Not mine. It's a start," El said, already pushing it live.

It went live.

He let out a breath, crossed to the bench, and lifted the peach from where he had set it down, turning it to the light to check the blush along the seam. It had ripened exactly on cue.

"There," he said, pleased. "You see? That one came out kind."

The angel still had the document open: the fault, the behavior, the patch, and a field at the bottom waiting for a word.

"How do I log it?"

El did not look up from the peach.

"Working as intended."
